# Story style review

24 September 2026. All 12 articles and 13 recipes checked against:

- **The studio's writing rules:** plain language, "we" voice, human moment first and the study second, paragraphs of at most 3 to 4 sentences, family moments used sparingly, no slang, no AI-tell words, no dashes.
- **Your health copy rule:** claims about water are fine when attributed with "we believe" framing. No claims about disease, healing, immunity or toxin removal.

- **Every article reads harder than the studio's target.** Reading level runs from about Year 11 to university. "A busy parent should understand it immediately" is closer to Year 8 to 9. Even the newest stories run long, mainly because the sentences average 23 to 32 words.
- **Four articles carry real claim risk** under your rule. Two of them promote practitioners or products with healing and immunity claims.
- **No AI-tell words anywhere.** Slang appears in two stories: "stuff" and "bugs".
- **Recipes are mostly fine.** Only one claim needs a small change (below).

## Priority 1: rewrite

### How your teeth affect your entire body
The most "doctor" of them all. It stacks jargon: cytokines, TNF-alpha, IL-6, bidirectional pathways, biomarkers.

Claims to remove or reframe:
- "the body can heal itself"
- gum disease described as "immune events"
- "supporting the immune system of every person in your household"
- speeding up arthritis, heart disease and diabetes, and links to Alzheimer's
- ozone therapy "designed to support immune health"

Credibility risk: "Quantum dentistry", "energetic pathways" and the named practitioner's "Quantum Kid" read as endorsement of an unproven practice. That undercuts the evidence-based tone and would be a problem if anyone complained.

### You are what you eat: fermented foods
Written as "I" rather than "we", with clipped fragments ("Not as a saying. As a biological reality.") and the slang "bugs".

Claims:
- "boosts" diversity, "regulate immune responses and reduce inflammation throughout the whole body"
- "measurable immune improvements through food"
- disease lists (Parkinson's, COPD, diabetes)

**Highest risk:** it promotes Robert Gourlay's Phi'on products, saying they "support the body's natural capacity to regulate and heal at a cellular level", and quotes him on living "without disease". Healing claims tied to a named commercial product are exactly what the TGA acts on. This needs your call on how to present Robert.

### The quiet deficiency (magnesium)
The hardest to read (grade 15), with dense clauses like "subclinical inflammation", "oxidative stress" and "microbiota-gut-brain axis".

Claims: links to anxiety, depression, diabetes, heart disease and bowel disease. Most are fairly attributed ("associated with"), and it already says magnesium is not a remedy. But "the research is not tentative about it" overstates, and the list of deficiency "signs" reads like diagnosis. A plain-language pass should soften it.

### 10 proven ways to build lasting health
Long sentences.

Claims: sunlight and vitamin D lowering the risk of "several cancers, autoimmune conditions"; household toxins "strongly linked to increased risk of cancer"; stress causing "immune dysfunction". The toxin line in particular falls under your no-toxin-claims rule.

## Priority 2: plain-language pass, light claim edits

- **Structured water:** your flagship topic and the longest story, with 9 paragraphs over the limit. Claims are already carefully attributed and hedged ("does not prove that drinking structured water reverses disease"), which fits your water rule. It mainly needs shorter paragraphs and plainer science. The line "pathology, meaning disease at the cellular level, is consistently associated with a loss of EZ water" should be softened.
- **Restorative sleep:** good voice and family moments, but long. "The immune system ramps up its surveillance" and "immune resilience" are mild immunity claims; reword them as what happens during sleep rather than a promise.
- **Teenager strength:** clean, just dense. The claims are fairly stated ("associated with reduced risk").
- **Red light therapy:** short and fine. Soften "inflammation markers".

## Priority 3: small touch-ups

- **Feet:** follows the rules well but has the longest sentences on the site (32 words on average). Split a few.
- **Beef tallow (both):** already in the house voice. Shorten the long paragraphs. The heart disease line is fairly worded.
- **Hidden seed oils:** plain and practical, but written as "I", says "stuff", and signs off as "Pristine Wellness" instead of Mr or Mrs Wellness. Switch it to "we" and fix the author.

## Recipes
