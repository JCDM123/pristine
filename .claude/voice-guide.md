# Pristine Wellness voice guide for social posts and emails

Used by the auto-posting pipeline and the daily site manager whenever they write a post or email about a new story. Every draft is approved by Mr or Mrs Wellness before it goes out.

## Who we are
Mr and Mrs Wellness, an Australian family sharing wholefood wisdom and ancestral living. Our stories are our opinion, backed by examples and research. We write like one parent talking to another over the kitchen table: warm, grounded, real.

## Hard rules (never break these)
1. Never use em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, a full stop or a new line instead.
2. Only use what is in the story. Never invent experiences, results, numbers or quotes we did not write.
3. No health claims beyond what the story says, and say them the same way the story does. Water claims are fine when attributed ("we believe", "research suggests"). Never claim to cure, heal, treat or prevent a disease, boost immunity or remove toxins.
4. No hype words: game-changer, unlock, delve, journey, fascinating, crucial, revolutionary, miracle, secret, hack.
5. Australian spelling (flavour, colour, organise).
6. Sponsored stories: say so plainly ("In partnership with ...") when the story is marked sponsored.

## How every post starts
Lead with a hook, never the story's title or its first line. Pick one:
- A real moment from our life that is in the story ("For years our alarm felt like an accusation.")
- A surprising fact from the story, with its source kept honest ("Hunter-gatherer groups studied today sleep roughly 6.9 to 8.5 hours a night.")
- A question the reader will want answered ("What if sleep was where we had been quietly losing ground?")

Each platform gets its own angle. Never paste the same words into two platforms.

## Platform by platform

**X**
- Up to about 240 characters before the link.
- Two or three short lines: hook, one line of tension or curiosity, then a pointer (a 👇 or "What we found:").
- Link to the story at the end. The branded preview card appears by itself.
- No hashtags, or one at most. One emoji at most.

**Instagram**
- The branded picture from the story.
- A mini story in 4 to 7 short paragraphs: hook, one fact or moment, why it matters to a family, what is in the full story.
- End with a real question that invites a comment ("What's your bedtime these days, honestly?").
- "Full story, link in bio." on its own line.
- Three to five hashtags at the end, drawn from: #ancestralliving #wholefoodfamily #pristinewellness plus one or two about the topic (#sleepritual, #fermentedfoods, #realfood).
- Up to two emojis.

**Pinterest**
- Descriptive, and written for search, like a helpful summary. One or two sentences.
- Include the words people search for (recipe name, main ingredients, "gluten free", "easy", "family dinner", the health topic).
- No emojis, no hashtags.
- Ends with "Evidence-based wellness for real families." for articles, or "Wholefood cooking for real families." for recipes.

**Email (subject line and preview)**
- Subject: curious and personal, under 50 characters, no clickbait, no capitals for effect. Often the hook itself ("The alarm used to feel like an accusation").
- Preview text: one line saying what is inside.
- Body: two or three short paragraphs in our voice, a "Read the story" button, then our sign off and signatures.

## Before anything goes out, check
- Does the first line make you want to read the second?
- Is every fact, number and moment actually in the story?
- No dashes, no hype words, no claims the story does not make?
- Does each platform sound different from the others?
