The Experiment

We document what we observe,
honestly and in real time.

Our ancestors drank water that came to them the way nature made it, tumbling down waterfalls and running over stone, carrying the frequencies of the living world it passed through. Most of the water we drink today, whether from the town supply or a bottle, has lost that. It arrives positively charged and structurally flat.

The MEA water device, designed by Robert Gourlay, is built to return that water to its original state: negatively charged, restructured, closer to the water our bodies evolved to drink.

This page is where we test that claim in the open. No laboratory, no funding, no agenda. We put structured MEA water beside regular town water and simply watch what happens. Below you will find our live water test, with a new photo each time we check the jars, and a collection of before and after experiments from around our home.

Blueberries: Structured MEA Water vs Filtered Town Water

One of the simplest ways to confirm that water has been converted by the MEA Structured Water Device is through a blueberry preservation test. The blueberries used in this experiment were bottled on 25 January 2024. The first photograph (left) was taken just one month later, on 22 February 2024, while the second photograph was taken on 2 May 2025, more than 15 months after bottling. Despite the extended period, the blueberries preserved in the structured water environment continue to show remarkable stability, providing a clear visual demonstration of the water's unique properties. The images allow you to compare the condition of the berries over time and observe the long-term effects for yourself.

Live Blood Tests with MEA structured water

Live blood tests were undertaken by a certified practitioner of the Institute of Functional Medicine, Australia. The participants were healthy and followed an 80% organic diet. The tests were undertaken 30-40 minutes after drinking ½ cup of MEA structured water. The test results show significant improvement in blood cell separation. This is due to the increased negative charge of the blood cells and separation by negative charge repulsion from the cell membrane. Images by Alexandra Brewster, IFMCP (US) MHS (UNE Aust)
